# P-01 Delivery and Scoring Guide (INTERNAL — for an English-speaking helper/reviewer)

This guide is not a prerequisite reading assignment for the beginner. The
actual learner lessons are `content/A0/A0-M1-U1-L01.md` and
`content/A0/A0-M1-U1-L02.md`. Both contain all 21 template sections, supplied
pictures, complete worked exchanges, response spaces and keys. This guide
records details that must not become untaught English instructions in them.

## 1. Honest delivery conditions

- **Zero prior English is assumed**, including no instruction vocabulary and
  no English letter names. Start with the nonverbal demonstration strip in
  L01 §1, not an English explanation of how to study.
- **Visual meaning and writing:** the supplied SVGs and worked responses are
  usable without understanding any advanced section-title words: the actual
  learner headings contain only symbols/IDs and already-taught language. Tracing copies visible
  shapes; it does not presuppose knowing the alphabet or spelling a name.
- **Sound:** there are **no audio recordings**. For pronunciation and real
  listening, a competent English-speaking helper must supply the model.
  Mouth drawings and optional IPA cannot teach every sound unaided. A solo
  learner can use the visual/reading/writing route, but reading a script is
  not listening and earns no listening evidence. Fully autonomous zero-to-
  spoken-English delivery remains incomplete until suitable audio exists.
- Lesson duration **50–70 minutes** is an untrialled estimate, including
  pauses. It is not a time limit; stop and resume as needed.
- The pictures are actual original SVG files under `content/A0/assets/p01/`.
  English alt text and SVG descriptions assist helpers/accessibility, but
  have not been established as a zero-English screen-reader-only route.

## 2. Bootstrap the actions without English instruction words

Use the actual five-row `actions.svg` strip; demonstrate silently first.

| Row | Visible demonstration | What the learner then does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eye directed at a triangle; the same triangle singled out | Attend to the supplied picture/word; point at its referent |
| 2 | One open-mouthed speaker makes a sound; a second speaker imitates it | Hear your model, then imitate it aloud; no word such as *listen*, *say* or *repeat* is required |
| 3 | A circle model becomes a copied circle beside a pencil | Trace/copy visible shapes or text onto paper |
| 4 | A circle model; circle/triangle options; only the circle marked | Select a single matching answer; this is demonstrated before any language choice task |
| 5 | Visible card, covered card, recalled content, uncovered match and tick | Cover models/keys, retrieve, then uncover to compare |

Six small icons repeat those actions (eye, mouth, pencil, selected response,
covered card, ear). The ear is paired with the helper-to-learner scene in
§9; it does not silently presume that the learner knows *listen*. A pencil
or an exit may be drawn without teaching the English nouns *pencil* or
*door*: no instruction or answer requires naming those objects.

introductions are corrected from L01 to L02.

**Grammar:** G003 supports the one main L01 identity frame G004. G011 is
limited to my/your + name in L02; G010 is a whole-chunk preview of the exact
name question. The broader topic rows are NOT permission to use it is,

not a logical prerequisite of I/you; G001 begins with symbols in L05.

## 4. Sound model and pronunciation guidance

The lesson diagrams show visible movements and rhythm; IPA below is for a
competent helper, not something the beginner must decode. Use a natural,
clear pace, then repeat more slowly without distorting the words. Learners
need not copy one accent. The diagrams use a non-rhotic British reference;
intelligible rhotic variants of are/your are also acceptable.

| Word | Reference | Model and visible cue |
|---|---|---|
| hello | /həˈləʊ/ | Two syllables; stronger second beat. Gentle initial breath; tongue tip behind upper teeth for l; vowel glides towards rounded lips. |
| hi | /haɪ/ | Gentle h breath before the open-to-narrow vowel glide. |
| goodbye | /ˌɡʊdˈbaɪ/ | Model the complete /ɡʊd/ first, then the stronger /baɪ/. The drawn closed lips represent b after that first syllable, NOT the initial g. The added phoneme cue makes this distinction explicit. |
| bye | /baɪ/ | Lips closed for b, release into the same vowel movement as I. |
| I | /aɪ/ | One syllable, open-to-narrow vowel glide, with no h or b. |
| you | /juː/ | A brief y-like glide into a sustained rounded vowel. |
| am | /æm/ | Front open vowel, then close both lips for m; do not omit the final closed-lip sound. Full form first, natural weak form later. |
| are | /ɑː/ | Sustained open vowel in the reference model; an audible r in rhotic accents is not an error. |
| name | /neɪm/ | Tongue tip for n, vowel glide, closed-lip m. One syllable. |
| my | /maɪ/ | Closed lips for m, then the familiar I vowel glide. |
| your | /jɔː/ | Brief glide into a sustained rounded vowel; normal regional/rhotic variants accepted. |
| is | /ɪz/ | Short vowel then voiced z. Let the learner feel their own throat vibration; do not touch their throat. Do not model a final s. |
| what | /wɒt/ | Rounded starting lips, open vowel, then tongue-tip t; acceptable regional vowel/t variants are not penalised. |

Model **I am Anna / You are Tom**, **My name is Anna / Your name is Tom** and
**What is your name?** as connected phrases, without inserting extra vowels.
The question's neutral model can fall at the end; do not teach that every
English question rises. Full forms are deliberate; contractions are not new
instructional targets in P-01. IPA recognition is never tested.

## 5. Actual listening scripts and delivery

Each lesson §9 contains the four actual numbered script lines under a
collapsed disclosure, with four matching tasks above it. Keep the text out
of the learner's view, read line 1 twice, allow a response, then lines 2–4
the same way. Reread on request by gesture; there is no scored time limit.
Use neutral delivery, not gestures that reveal the answer. The learner has
already learned every script word in §4.

- L01: scene meaning for lines 1–2; speaker/listener frame discrimination
  for lines 3–4. Keys: P1, P2, I am Anna, You are Tom.
- L02: my/your discrimination, recognition of the complete name request,
  then the identity frame from the final utterance. Keys: My, Your,
  What is your name?, My name is Tom.
- With no helper: the same text is usable for **reading rehearsal only**.
  Never mark audio verified or a listening item assessed from silent reading.
- At this stage there are no unsupported abstract inference questions.
  L01 reading sequence and L02 speaker/label reference provide supported
  reasoning within the pictured context instead.

## 6. Task solving, alternatives and scoring

Both keys are inside the actual lessons (§18). There are **39 + 47 = 86**
keyed prompts, including the six L02 cold-retrieval prompts, all skill
practice, recall and the two eight-item Lesson Checks. Worked models E01–E07
precede the exercises. Review tasks reference these real, keyed prompt IDs.

- Closed choices: one available correct option each; no instruction word
  is itself being tested. `audit_pilot_batch.py` derives the answers from
  student content before reading the keys.
- Open greeting/farewell tasks accept both taught synonyms. A supplied
  addressee is not an independent vocabulary/spelling target.
- In L02 B04, **My name is Anna**, **I am Anna**, and the bare label **Anna**
  are all natural responses. A bare name is communicatively correct, but
  does not demonstrate the new full frame. This distinction must not become
  "your English is wrong" feedback.
- Personal identity tasks accept any actual name, or a chosen pictured role.
  If the learner cannot yet write their actual name in Latin script, a
  copied Anna/Tom badge is an offered practice route, not a compulsory new
  English identity. Never grade a name's meaning, choice or spelling.
- Where a full frame is assessed, the student prompt cues it: L01 Q07 has
  **I ___ ___.**, Q08 has **You ___ Tom.**, and L02 Q08 has
  **My name ___ ___.** The learner says/writes the whole requested frame.
- Naturally produced **I'm…**, **You're…**, **My name's…** or **What's your
  name?** are valid English alternatives. Accept correct contractions if
  independently produced; they are not modeled, required or counted as
  additional new words in these lessons. Unmodeled natural answers are not
  evidence that they have been taught to everyone.

### Embedded check rubric (both QZs)

Eight items, one point each. Q01–Q06 use the printed keys. Q07 is oral and
Q08 is written. **Six points are needed**: ceil(70% × 8) = 6, so the actual
minimum whole-item score is **75%**, not "exactly 70%".

| Item | 1 point | 0 points | Not assessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| L01 Q07 | Intelligible complete I am + speaker's identity (or natural contraction) | Required frame absent or wrong am/are link after a heard attempt | No competent listener/retained observation or recording |
| L01 Q08 | Complete You are Tom frame; identity understood from the badge | Wrong link or missing required frame | No written response retained |
| L02 Q07 | Intelligible complete name request (or natural contraction) | Required request not produced or wrong order preventing the taught request | No competent listener/retained observation or recording |
| L02 Q08 | Complete My name is + identity frame | Missing/wrong is or required frame absent | No written response retained |

Accent, speed, name choice/spelling, capitals and punctuation are NOT scored
here. Correctly model capitals and punctuation, but their independent rule
teaching belongs to G002 later. A question mark and badge in a picture are
meaning support, not a punctuation test.

The student score display uses ✓/✗/? and ?/8. A missing Q07 judgement is
**not zero and not an automatic pass**: no complete total is recorded until
all eight have evidence. No scores or learner successes have been entered
for these materials. Same-item correction is practice, not a scored retake;
a parallel form must exist before a scored retake is offered.

## 7. Recall and adaptation

L01 §15 has six real retrieval prompts; L02 §15 has seven, including L01
items. Cover examples, banks and keys before recalling; uncover afterwards.
L02 starts with six cold prompts **before new language**. The last two
require whole I am / You are lines, rather than merely rereading I/you.

The supplied sun–moon–sun diagram grounds the day symbol without the word
*day*. The schedule is adaptive: next lesson, then **+1, +3, +7, +21, +60
days after the last successful retrieval**, not six fixed dates all counted
from the introduction. Any failure schedules +1 day again and repair in
§4/§6/§11/§12. Each table names exact existing tasks and alternates speech,
written gap-filling and personal production. No success is prefilled.

L02's next-lesson retrieval is reserved for L03 §1. This is a schedule only:
**L03 and the next batch have not been authored or started.**
